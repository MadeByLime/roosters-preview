A cracked glass top is not cosmetic.

That sheet of glass is the only thing between you and the elements and wiring underneath, so a crack or a chip lets a spill reach live parts. Stop using the surface altogether rather than steering round the cracked zone, and cut the power at the breaker. A cracked top gets replaced, never patched.

Tell us what you see.

Gas, radiant glass and induction fail in different ways, so each symptom below names the kind it applies to. Work through the check first. Several of these never need a technician at all.

  • One burner will not light

    Gas. The rest of the burners work normally.

    Try this first. Lift the cap and the head off that burner and look at the ports. Food boils over and sets hard in them, and a blocked ring will not carry a flame. Wash both parts in warm soapy water, clear each port with a straightened paperclip, dry them fully and seat the cap flat. A cap sitting proud by a millimetre stops ignition on its own.

    Clean, dry and properly seated and it still will not light? That burner’s igniter or its valve has gone, and both sit under the top.

    Tell us what it's doing

  • Clicking that will not stop

    Gas. Sparking carries on after the burner has lit.

    Try this first. Moisture is the usual answer. Dry around every igniter and under every cap, leave the cooktop off for an hour, then try again. Check that each knob is fully off rather than a hair past the mark. A cooktop still sparking with everything dry and every knob off should be switched off at the breaker.

    That is a cracked igniter or a failed spark module. It arcs next to gas until it is replaced, so it is worth moving on quickly.

    Tell us what it's doing

  • The glass is cracked

    Radiant or induction. A chip, a crack or a hole in the surface.

    Try this first. There is nothing to attempt here and nothing to clean. Cut the power at the breaker, keep liquids off it and stop using the surface. Measure the cutout in your counter and find the model number, because a replacement has to match both.

    The top is replaced as a unit. We price the glass against the price of the whole cooktop and say plainly when replacing the appliance makes more sense.

    Tell us what it's doing

  • Induction will not see the pan

    Induction. The zone beeps, flashes and shuts itself off.

    Try this first. Hold a fridge magnet to the base of the pan. If it does not grip firmly, induction cannot heat it, and the pan is the problem rather than the cooktop. Aluminium, copper and a lot of stainless will not work. The base also has to be flat and wide enough for the zone, roughly two thirds of its diameter, so a small pot on a big ring gets ignored.

    A magnetic pan of the right size refused on every zone means the coil or the power board under the glass.

    Tell us what it's doing

  • Only works on high

    Radiant or gas. The burner cycles hard, or will not drop to a simmer.

    Try this first. On a radiant top some of this is normal. The elements have no low flame, so they hold a low setting by switching on and off, and you see the glow come and go. Running at full power on every setting is not normal. On gas, a burner that will not simmer usually needs its low-flame screw set, which sits inside the valve.

    An element stuck on full is a failed infinite switch or a shorted relay. Both are short jobs once the top is lifted.

    Tell us what it's doing

  • Controls are dead

    Any type. The touch panel ignores you, or nothing powers up.

    Try this first. Look for the lock first. Nearly every glass cooktop has a child lock on a padlock symbol, held three seconds, and it is easy to set while wiping the surface. Then dry the panel properly, because a film of water reads as a finger held down and the controls shut themselves off. Last, flip the breaker fully off and back, both halves of it.

    Dry, unlocked, breaker reset and still nothing means the touch board or the filter board underneath, and those come out from the cabinet below.

Before youpick up the phone.

A cooktop tends to lose one zone at a time, which is why people live with it for months. Here is what they ask when they finally call.

Is my cooktop worth repairing?

A burner, an igniter, a valve, an element or a switch is almost always worth fixing. A cracked glass top or a failed induction board on a mid-range unit often is not, because either part alone can approach the price of a new cooktop. We tell you which one you are looking at before you decide.

How long does a cooktop repair take?

Many are done on the first visit, because igniters, elements and switches are what the van carries. When a part has to be ordered it runs three to five business days, then a return trip. You will know which one before the tech leaves.

Do you work on induction?

Yes, alongside gas and radiant. Induction fails differently, usually at the coil, the cooling fan or the power board rather than at anything you can see from above, so it gets its own set of tests. Have the model number ready when you call.

Can a cracked glass top be repaired?

No. The glass is one sealed piece and it gets replaced whole, so there is nothing to patch or fill. Stop using the surface and cut the power until it is swapped, because a crack lets spills through to the elements and the wiring.

What does the diagnostic visit include?

A flat $140 covers the trip and the full diagnosis. The tech tests every zone, the igniters or elements, the switches and the control board, and on gas reads the flame at each burner. You get the cause and a repair price on the spot, and the $140 comes off the labor if you go ahead.

Is the repair guaranteed?

Ninety days, covering the parts and the labor both. Parts are genuine manufacturer parts from licensed suppliers, nothing aftermarket. If the same fault turns up again inside that window, we come back and you pay nothing more.

Do you have to take the cooktop out?

For most repairs, yes. A drop-in cooktop sits in a cutout with all the working parts underneath, so it gets unclipped and lifted clear to reach them, then goes back sealed to the counter. The exception is a gas burner cap or head, which lifts off from above in a minute.

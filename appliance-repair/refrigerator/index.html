A warm fridge goes first.

A fridge that quits in the morning is spoiled groceries by night. Nothing else in the house costs you money that fast. So a fridge call takes the nearest slot on the board, and we push to get a tech there ASAP.

Sound familiar?

Six things a fridge does before it costs you a week of groceries. Start with the checks we'd run first. If that isn't it, it's ours.

  • Fridge isn't cooling

    Food isn't staying cold, but the light and the fans still work.

    Try this first. Clear anything pressed against the vents inside the fridge and the freezer, and stop packing it wall to wall. Then unplug the unit and vacuum the condenser coils, usually behind a grille at the floor or along the back. Dust and pet hair on those coils are enough on their own to stop a fridge cooling. Set the fridge to 40°F or below and the freezer to 0°F, and give it a full day to settle.

    Still warm the next morning? Then it is the evaporator fan, the condenser fan, the compressor or the start relay, and none of those is a kitchen job.

    Tell us what it's doing

  • Water is leaking

    Water pooling under the fridge or collecting inside it.

    Try this first. Start with the water filter. If it was changed in the last month, pull it out and seat it again until it locks. A filter that isn't fully home, or an off-brand one that doesn't match the housing, drips all day. Then pull the fridge out, look at the fitting where the supply line connects, and run a level across the top. A unit tilted forward tips water out of its own drain pan.

    Water collecting inside, under the crispers, means the defrost drain is blocked. Reaching it means the rear freezer panel comes off, so leave that one to us.

  • Ice maker stopped

    No ice, slow ice, or the ice maker won't cycle.

    Try this first. Most manufacturers want the freezer between 0 and 5°F before the ice maker will cycle at all, so check that first. Make sure the arm or the switch is on. Hold the reset button a few seconds, or unplug the fridge for one minute if yours has no reset. If the filter is past six months, replace it. If you just replaced it, take it out and push it back until it clicks.

    No ice after a day of that and it's the fill valve, a frozen fill tube or the module itself. That is what we come out for.

  • Freezer is frosting

    Ice builds up in the freezer or on the back wall.

    Try this first. Frost starts with warm air getting in. Check that nothing on a shelf or in a drawer is holding the door off its seal, then run a finger round the gasket for grit or a split and wash it with warm water and dish soap. Never chip at the ice with a knife or a screwdriver. The liner and the coil sit right behind it.

    An even sheet of frost on the back wall is a different problem. That's the defrost cycle not running: heater, thermostat or sensor. Call us before the airflow goes with it.

    Tell us what it's doing

  • Loud or nonstop running

    Constant noise, humming, or the unit never shuts off.

    Try this first. A fridge that never shuts off usually can't dump its heat. Unplug it, vacuum the condenser coils, and leave two inches of clear air behind the cabinet. Push on the corners, and if it rocks, bring the front feet down until it sits solid. Then wait a day. A fridge you just filled with groceries on a hot afternoon will run long, and that is it doing its job.

    Loud a day later means a fan motor or the compressor, and that is worth diagnosing before it takes the food with it.

    Tell us what it's doing

  • Door won't seal

    Door is sweating or not sealing, so it won't hold temperature.

    Try this first. Close a dollar bill in the door and pull. If it slides out with no drag, the seal has let go at that spot. Before you order a gasket, wash it with warm water and dish soap, because grease and crumbs alone will break a seal. A stiff gasket that is otherwise sound often comes back with a thin smear of petroleum jelly. Last thing, check the fridge sits a touch lower at the front so the door falls shut by itself.

    If the bill still slides after all that, the gasket is finished or a hinge has dropped. Both are short visits.

Good questions.Straight answers.

A refrigerator rarely dies all at once. Here are the questions we hear most from Bay Area homeowners.

Is my refrigerator worth repairing?

It depends on the fault and the unit. A fan, a gasket, a defrost part, or an ice maker is almost always worth fixing. A failed compressor on a low-end fridge often is not, and we will tell you that rather than sell you a repair.

How long does a refrigerator repair take?

Some are done on the first visit. The van carries a small stock of the parts that fail most often, but more often the part has to be ordered, which takes three to five business days before a return trip. You will know which it is before the tech leaves.

Do you repair built-in refrigerators?

Yes, including Sub-Zero, and we are an authorized Bertazzoni service provider. Built-ins use model-specific parts, so call with the model number and we will confirm coverage in a minute.

What does the diagnostic visit include?

A flat $140 covers the trip and the full diagnosis: temperature and airflow readings, the defrost cycle, and the sealed system. You get the cause and a repair price on the spot, and the $140 comes off the labor if you go ahead.

Is the repair guaranteed?

Ninety days, covering the parts and the labor both. Parts are genuine manufacturer parts from licensed suppliers, nothing aftermarket. If the same fault turns up again inside that window, we come back and you pay nothing more.

Can you just recharge the refrigerant?

Only after fixing the leak. A sealed system does not lose charge unless something is leaking, so topping it up without the repair fails again within weeks.

My food keeps freezing in the fridge. Why?

Usually a stuck air damper or a failing thermostat or sensor, sometimes a blocked vent or a setting that is simply too low. We find which one instead of guessing.
